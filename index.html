At a glance

B.S. Geophysics (Honors) at the University of Texas at Austin, Jackson School of Geosciences, with a Certificate in Computational Science & Engineering from the Oden Institute. I've been a research associate at the University of Texas Institute for Geophysics (UTIG) since 2022, and I was on UTIG's airborne radar team during the 2025–26 Antarctic field season.

Next, I plan to pursue research that keeps one foot in geoscience and one in ecology, giving a voice to ecological underdogs: species too misunderstood, cryptic, or remote to register against the whims of industrial expansion. My computational and geoscience background lets me ask how Earth systems shape ecosystems, and how ecosystems shape them in return.

Outside the lab, I host a long-form science interview podcast. I'm happiest in places where the work and the landscape are both a little extreme.

02Fieldwork

Dec 2025 – Feb 2026 · RV Araon · UTIG & KOPRI

Airborne radar over Thwaites Glacier

I was a field technician on UTIG's four-person airborne ice-penetrating radar team during an international expedition to the Amundsen Sea Embayment aboard the Korean icebreaker RV Araon. I planned helicopter radar surveys, helped the senior radar engineer run and troubleshoot the instrument, and processed and quality-checked radar data in real time.

Over six survey flights we flew roughly 3,000 line-km of multi-polarization radar over the Thwaites, Haynes and Crosson glaciers, recording every line in both polarizations. The lines included the first helicopter survey of Haynes Glacier and the first look at the largely unsurveyed Crosson Ice Shelf. The data target ice-shelf damage, crystal fabric, grounding-line change and support for the BAS/KOPRI hot-water drill camp. Fractured floating ice at Thwaites is also a terrestrial analog for Europa, where our group's REASON instrument aboard Europa Clipper will sound the ice shell.

The work was covered by The New York Times and PBS NewsHour.
