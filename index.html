UTIG · 2022 – present

Why bed DEMs matter: geothermal heat at Haynes Glacier

Bed topography is the most important boundary condition for an ice model, but widely used bed products (Bedmap, BedMachine) carry large uncertainties and some circular assumptions. I compare interpolation approaches, including ordinary kriging, stochastic simulation of bed roughness, and mass-balance-informed topography. Then I trace how each choice carries through to shallow-ice-approximation model output.

Haynes Glacier makes a sharp test case. Dipping englacial reflectors in the radar data point to enhanced basal melt from anomalous, spatially variable geothermal flux, likely linked to nearby volcanic centers such as Mt. Takahe.

Honors thesis

Subglacial volcanism beneath Thwaites Glacier

I'm testing whether subglacial volcanism can be detected in ice-penetrating radar, and how it might affect shear-margin mechanics in the Thwaites catchment. The work combines IPR observations with thermally coupled ice-flow models (shallow ice approximation and 3D finite-difference Stokes flow).

  • Thwaites Glacier
  • Stokes flow
  • Thermomechanics

Oden Institute · Advisor: Dr. Timothy Keitt

Rivers, birdsong & the Amazon

In this independent project, I'm using BirdNET, an open-source machine-learning tool, to map variation in antbird (Thamnophilidae) song across geographic space. I'm asking whether that variation tracks the geomorphological evolution of Amazonian rivers, and whether it can help with population assessment.

  • Machine learning
  • Bioacoustics
  • Geomorphology

02Fieldwork

Dec 2025 – Feb 2026 · RV Araon · UTIG & KOPRI

Airborne radar over Thwaites Glacier

I was a field technician on UTIG's four-person airborne ice-penetrating radar team during an international expedition to the Amundsen Sea Embayment aboard the Korean icebreaker RV Araon. I planned helicopter radar surveys, helped the senior radar engineer run and troubleshoot the instrument, and processed and quality-checked radar data in real time.

The season set a record for inland radar coverage, made new measurements of ice crystal-fabric orientation, and imaged subglacial terrain that had never been mapped. The work was covered by The New York Times and PBS NewsHour.
